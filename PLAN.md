# Saint Germain des Fleurs — Research & Plan

> **Saint Germain des Fleurs** is a small florist shop on place Saint-Germain, behind the Église
> Saint-Germain in Rennes' historic centre. It is the town shop of a family flower farm, **La Ferme aux
> Mille Fleurs**, in Plouézec (Côtes-d'Armor). Horticulteur Jean-Michel Leven sold for years at Rungis
> and then at the Saturday **Marché des Lices**. The shop opened on **17 May 2020**, when the COVID
> lockdown closed the markets. It sells **local, seasonal flowers** (« Fleurs locales de saison »):
> ready-made, custom and pick-your-own bouquets, plus dried-flower bouquets and wreaths. The farm grows
> outdoors in one tunnel on 5 ha, with no heated greenhouse and no chemical inputs. Name styling: the
> sources write "Saint Germain des Fleurs" (no hyphens, capital G), and the Instagram handle is
> `@saintgermaindesfleurs`. One actu.fr piece writes "Saint-Germain des Fleurs". **No other Rennes business
> has this name, so the identity is unambiguous.**

## 0. Status & confidence

- Date: **2026-10-13**. This was a **fast research pass by a single agent, with no separate fact-check**. I used about 24 searches and fetches.
- Identity: **high confidence**. Three independent sources agree on the name, the place, the phone and the Leven family link.
- Registry (SIREN/NAF/creation date): **not found**. The recherche-entreprises API returned nothing for the shop name, "Leven" or the farm name. The shop is probably registered under a person's or an EARL's name. See §12.

## 2. Story & history

- Jean-Michel Leven was first a **bûcheron** (woodcutter) and turned to horticulture "in response to demand". He ran the family farm with his wife and children. For about **15 years he sold to professionals at Rungis**, then moved to **Breton markets**. He was a fixture "au début du Marché des Lices" for about ten years ([u]).
- In the **COVID lockdown of spring 2020** the markets closed and flowers went unsold. Marie Leven said: « Ça faisait mal au cœur de devoir jeter des fleurs, nos pivoines, nos anémones… ». The family set up **temporary delivery in Rennes from mid-April** and used the farm's Instagram to « communiquer avec les clients ». Marie Leven again: « Les fleurs ne sont pas un service primaire, mais elles font du bien ». They decided not to go back to Rungis and **opened the shop on 17 May 2020** ([u]). Unidivers' tagline was « Rançon du succès : c'est une nouvelle fleur qui a éclos. »
- **Later:** Marie Leven, who started at 15 by making bouquets from her father's market flowers (« J'ai commencé à 15 ans… Mon père vendait des fleurs sur le marché et moi, je les mettais en bouquet »), went back to Plouézec with **Margot Le Bars**. They grow and open the farm on Thursdays. Jean-Michel is described as a « producteur d'hortensias et autres fleurs non loin du moulin de Craca » ([ac]).
- The story is **farm → market → shop**: the flowers travel from a coastal field in Goëlo (Côtes-d'Armor) to a Rennes square. This is the spine of the site.

## 8. Design direction

**Concept: « De Plouézec à Saint-Germain » (*Le Chemin de la tige*).** The site follows a stem's journey: it is cut at dawn in a coastal field, rides 150-odd km of road *(approximate; compute it at build time and do not print an unverified figure)*, sits on the Lices market stall, and ends up on the counter at place Saint-Germain. The site is also a **living seasonal calendar**. The catalogue, hero bloom and accent colour follow the *current month*, because the shop really only sells what is in season.

- **Palette:** Lin, background `#F3EEE4`; Granit, ink, from Breton granite and church schist, `#2F2D2A`; Kraft wrap `#C9A27E`; Ellébore, sage foliage, `#5E6F4E`; Hortensia, dusty blue, `#6C7FB0`; Renoncule, accent, `#D9572B`, used for large text and fills only, with `#B0421C` for small text and links to keep ≥ 4.5:1 on Lin; Dahlia lie-de-vin `#7A2E3A`. A **seasonal accent token** `--saison` cycles: renoncule (spring), pivoine `#D88A9A` (May–June), hortensia (summer), dahlia (autumn) and ellébore (winter).
- **Type:** **Fraunces**, a soft variable serif, for display. Its "WONK" and "SOFT" axes animate subtly, like petals opening. **Instrument Sans** is for body text. **IBM Plex Mono** is used sparingly for *étiquettes de marché*, the market-label tags (« Plouézec · 22 · cueilli mardi »).
- **Signature interaction, « Composer à la tige »:** the user builds a bouquet from a **bench of stems that only shows what is in season this month**. Out-of-season stems sit greyed out with a tag such as « revient en avril ». Dragged or tapped stems drop into a kraft wrap with gentle spring physics and fan out naturally. The result yields a `mailto:`/`tel:` order brief (« Bonjour, je souhaiterais un bouquet avec… »). It works with no backend, and the real ordering channel is phone or email. This is unique to this business: it puts « à composer » and « de saison » into practice.
- **Second signature, « La route »:** a pinned horizontal scroll section draws an SVG road. It goes from the Plouézec coast (moulin de Craca), through a Tuesday at the Paimpol market and a Saturday at the Lices, to the shop door on place Saint-Germain, with dawn-to-evening light shifting along the scrub.
- **Hero:** a single stem **grows with scroll**. An SVG path draws from soil to bloom, and the bloom is this month's flower. The wordmark splits and settles like petals around it. The idle state has a faint CSS wind sway.
- **Motion language, "pousse" (growth):** reveals come *upward* from the baseline (clip-path inset from the bottom) and go slow-in, soft-out. Nothing bounces except the bouquet physics. Use GSAP ScrollTrigger and SplitText with Lenis smooth scroll, and View Transitions between pages (a petal-wipe mask). A month-wheel on the « Saisons » page scrubs through the year. Animate transform and opacity only.
- **Reduced motion:** stems are shown fully grown and still, the route becomes a vertical illustrated timeline, the composer becomes an accessible checklist with a static bouquet preview, and there is no smooth scroll and no physics.
